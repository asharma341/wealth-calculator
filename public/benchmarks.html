Planning return

An assumption for many years

A benchmark is a starting point based on history or prevailing yields. Actual returns can be lower, negative or very different after fees, tax and currency changes.

Four reference points

Different assets do different jobs.

Higher return potential generally comes with more uncertainty. The best comparison includes risk, liquidity and time—not only the percentage.

Growth10%

Broad stocks

The S&P 500 has posted about a 10% annualised total return since its 1957 launch. Individual stocks and other countries can behave very differently.

  • Higher long-term growth potential
  • Large short-term declines possible
  • Best suited to longer horizons
S&P Dow Jones Indices
Diversifier8%

Gold

The World Gold Council reports roughly 8% annualised USD returns since 1971. Its future return can differ sharply from that history.

  • May diversify stock risk
  • No earnings or interest
  • Currency affects local returns
World Gold Council
Income6.9%

Government bonds

A reference near a recent India 10-year government-security yield. A bond’s actual return depends on price, duration and reinvestment.

  • Contractual interest payments
  • Prices move when rates change
  • Government and currency matter
Reserve Bank of India
Stability6.5%

Fixed deposits

A midpoint within a recent RBI range of 6.00%–6.75% for Indian bank term deposits over one year. Offers change by bank and term.

  • Known interest rate
  • Early-access limits may apply
  • Tax and inflation reduce real return
