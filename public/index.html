Know the assumptions

Four ideas that change how you read the result.

CAGR

Compound annual growth rate is the single annual rate that turns a starting value into an ending value over a period. It smooths the path: a 10% CAGR does not mean the investment earned exactly 10% every year.

CAGR = (ending ÷ beginning)^(1 ÷ years) − 1
Nominal vs. real return

Nominal return is the stated growth before inflation. Real return measures growth in purchasing power. The exact relationship is:

real return = (1 + nominal) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1
Taxes

Taxes depend on account type, residency, asset, holding period and jurisdiction. This calculator applies your tax percentage once to the projected gain; it does not model annual distributions, loss offsets or changing rules.

Sequence-of-returns risk

Two portfolios can have the same average return but different outcomes when money is added or withdrawn. Poor returns early in retirement can be especially damaging because withdrawals leave less capital to recover. This calculator uses a smooth return, so compare scenarios and keep a safety margin.

Estimates assume monthly compounding, contributions at the start of each month, and a constant return. Market data may be delayed. Educational planning tool—not financial, tax or investment advice.
